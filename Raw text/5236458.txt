Imagine joining a team where your work doesn’t just support digital products — it defines
how all of them are built.
At Alma Career, we are developing
Cyborg
, our internal backend‑for‑frontend framework that will power job portals used by millions across Europe. And now, we’re looking for someone who doesn’t just want to write code, but wants to
design how the framework itself should function
: shape its architecture, define its future, and make it a reliable foundation for teams across the company.
If you enjoy combining development with system‑level thinking — and you want your work to have a real, long‑term impact — this is an opportunity you won’t often find.
What will be your job?
our main responsibility will be working on the
development and optimization
of the Cyborg framework, continuously improving its performance, scalability, and overall architecture to support the evolving needs of the job board platform.
Our “users” are
developers
from other teams. We rarely work on visual components, or deal with business logic. We are here to provide them functionalities they need and use to build our job boards. Our focus is more on building stuff like router, service container, libraries and tools to make job boards modular. We are also responsible for preparing GitHub workflows and AWS infrastructure.
We work in a
Scrum-based environment
with two-week sprints, starting with planning and ending with a retrospective. We sync daily in morning stand-ups to share updates and align on next steps. Writing tests is integral part of each task and our code is subject for an inter-team code review.
On a quarterly basis, we define team goals together and distribute ownership across developers. As a developer with goal ownership, your role is not to deliver a goal alone, but to drive a topic forward, coordinate with others, and ensure the goal is successfully achieved.
How do we imagine you?
We’re searching for an engineer with h
ands‑on experience across multiple frameworks
and a strong foundation in modern web development. You should be comfortable thinking in systems, understanding how components fit together, and designing solutions that can scale across many products.
You’ll be a great match if you have:
Solid experience with
React
Strong skills in
JavaScript
Experience with
TypeScript
Good working knowledge of
Next.js
(ideally at a mid‑level or higher)
Some experience with other framework than just Next.js. Does not need to be a JavaScript framework, other language frameworks with service container are also fine (eg. Symfony)
Previous experience with AWS is a plus
We’re looking for someone who naturally gravitates toward architecture, framework design, and platform thinking.
Because you’ll work closely with teams who rely on Cyborg, we value people who can:
communicate clearly and openly,
bring well‑reasoned arguments to technical discussions,
actively listen and accept arguments from others,
collaborate effectively and seek solutions as a team,
contribute to a positive, constructive engineering culture.
You’ll also need
communicative English
, as you’ll use it regularly in our international environment.
Getting Started: What Awaits You
During first few weeks with us, you will be introduced to our architecture, processes and culture. We will try to gradually involve you in the development, starting with smaller and easier tasks.
Our plan for next months is to work on a routing system overhaul. Framework plugins can define their routes in configuration files inside their own NPM package, but Next.js has file system based routing. We need to create more flexible layer between those two.
We also want to bring more logging and debugging possibilities. And introduce better options regarding dependency injection of services registered in our service container.
At the same time, we would need to listen to other teams building another job board on our framework, help them with questions and provide occasional code review of their code.
Our team is small, less than 10 people. We are from different countries, so we work mostly remote. Once per three months, we meet in person in Bratislava, Prague, or Zagreb to define team goals for the next quarter. It usually takes two days and two to three nights.
